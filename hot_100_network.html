Promiscuity and The Billboard Hot 100

Assessing Songwriter Network Community Structures

Seth Berry

What Is A Network?

  • Networks are a subset of graph theory.

  • The idea is to model and analyze relationships between entities (nodes) and their connections (edges).

Networks Are Everywhere

  • Natural networks appear in biology, physics, and chemistry.

  • Social networks appear in sociology, economics, and organizational research.

  • Networks also appear in computer science, engineering, and telecommunications.

Prior Research

Networks and Organizational Functioning

  • A review of 187 articles by Chen et al. (2022) found 3 main categories of network dynamics research in organizations:

  1. Network Change: Emergence, evolution, and transformation of network ties and structures.

  2. Relational Events: Sequence of discrete actions generated by one actor and directed towards others.

  3. Coevolution: The network and actor attributes influence each other over time.

Networks and Innovation

  • Muller and Perez (2019) found that innovation growth is effect when networks follow the “3 Cs”:

    • Cohesion (strong mutual influence among its members)

    • Connectedness (high number of ties)

    • Conciseness (low redundancy)

Network Communities

  • Network communities are groups of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network.

  • Sytch and Tatarynowicz (2013) found that network communities can be a source increased patent productivity.

Community Promiscuity

  • Community promiscuity refers to the extent to which nodes are distributed across multiple communities within the network.

  • Promiscuity in the context of networks refers to the tendency of nodes to collaborate with a large number of different partners, rather than focusing on a small, stable group.

Finding Gaps in Promiscuity Research

  • While there has been research on network communities and their impact on innovation, there is a lack of research on the role of promiscuity in network structures.

Hypotheses

\(H_1\): Strong communities will appear within the songwriter network, indicating that certain groups of songwriters frequently collaborate with each other.

\(H_2\): Promiscuity will be normally distributed across the entire network.

\(H_3\): Songwriter performance will be positively related to promiscuity. Specifically, more promiscuous songwriters will have higher chart performance, as measured by the number of songs that reach the Billboard Hot 100.

Methods

Data Collection

  • Data was collected from the Billboard Hot 100 charts.

  • Data collection spanned from 1960 to 2026.

  • Songwriters, producers, chart entry position, and peak position were collected for each song.

Descriptive Statistics

  • The data contains 27138 unique songs, with 13791 nodes and 80303 edges.

Network Construction

  • An undirected, bipartite network was constructed with songwriters as nodes and edges representing collaborations.

  • Edges were weighted based on the number of collaborations between songwriters.

Community Detection

  • The Louvain method was applied to detect communities within the songwriter network.

  • Modularity was calculated to assess the strength of the detected communities.

  • Modularity is the fraction of the edges that fall within the given groups minus the expected fraction if edges were distributed at random.

  • Modularity values range from -1/2 to 1, with higher values indicating stronger community structure.

Detecting Promiscuity

  • Promiscuity was measured using the promiscuity index, which quantifies the extent to which nodes are distributed across multiple communities.

  • \(\psi_i = \frac{k_i}{K}\)

  • \(k_i\): The number of unique communities to which node i belongs.

  • \(K\): The total number of communities in the network.

  • A high score of 1 means the node participates in every single community in the network, while a low score means its allegiance is highly localized.

Results

Community Detection

  • 644 communities were detected within the songwriter network.

  • The modularity score of the detected communities was 0.602, indicating a strong level of community structure.

  • Average Clustering Coefficient: 0.730
